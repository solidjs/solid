A dormant lazy memo rejoins its owner's child chain when a read reawakens it (#3554). `unobserved()` splices an auto-dispose memo out of `parent._firstChild` when its last subscriber leaves, but the reawaken in `prepareComputed` only recomputed it, so the node ran live outside the chain with `_prevSibling === null` and a stale `_nextSibling`. Its next dormancy took the head branch of the splice and wrote that stale pointer into `parent._firstChild`, orphaning every sibling created after it: never disposed by the owner, still subscribed to their sources, still running after the root's `dispose()` (the dev-only `owner-chain-head` invariant from #3552 threw mid-flush instead). The reawaken now links the node back at the chain head before recomputing, so the owner's `dispose()` and reruns reach a reawakened memo again. Two nodes are not relinked: a zombie, because its splice was skipped and it still sits on the pending chain, and a memo whose owner is already disposed, because that owner's `dispose()` never drains its chain again and the splice that would remove the node at its next dormancy is skipped for a disposed parent, so the node stayed at the head and the next reawaken linked it to itself (`_nextSibling === _prevSibling === node`), which sent `markDisposal` and `DEV.getChildren` into an endless loop. A cleanup that reads such a memo while its owner's held children are torn down (`recompute` drains them in place without flagging the owner) relinks it the same way, and the drain's trailing `_firstChild = null` dropped it again, live and off the chain; `disposeChildren` now detaches the chain before its loop and points each child's splice at itself, so a node linked during the drain stays on the chain. The creation-time link in `createOwner` and `setupComputedNode` and the relink share one `linkChild` helper, which resets `_prevSibling` itself (core floor 25,671 -> 25,724 bytes).
