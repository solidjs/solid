# Async rules — the fundamentals

**Status: DRAFT for maintainer review (2026-10-07).** Not yet the fixed base. Drafted from every source named in the brief — `SPEC-ASYNC-SEMANTICS.md` and its amendments, `RULES-INDEX.md`, the `Loading`/`createLoadingBoundary`/`action`/`createOptimistic` JSDoc, the tests, the dev diagnostics, the pre-L2 recovery's eleven proposed rules, and the rulings of 2026-10-05 → 2026-10-07 (#3800, #3806, #3811, #3820, #3824, #3869/#3851, the dropped A35). Where two sources genuinely disagree the fundamental below does not decide; the disagreement is in [`OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`](./OPEN-QUESTIONS.md) as a screen sequence. Once the maintainer has reviewed this file once, each fundamental is **immutable**.

**IDs.** The new tiers are hyphenated — `F-1`, `D-12`, `S-3`, `Q-2` — so they never collide with the archive's bare ids (`A29`, `B5`) or with the semantic fuzzer's finding and signature names (`F6`, `F8`, `S1`, `S2`), which the archive and these files cite by those bare names.

**Precedence.** A fundamental beats a derived rule; a derived rule beats the archive. A derived rule _narrows_ how a fundamental applies to a specific shape; it never contradicts one. If a case seems to need a contradiction, it is not an amendment: it comes to the maintainer as a proposed change to the fundamental, in `OPEN-QUESTIONS.md`, as screens. A ruling that cannot point at a fundamental is flagged in `RULES-DERIVED.md` as a missing fundamental or a mistake — not quietly added.

**Why this shape.** Each ruling in the archive settled one case, and amendments patched the gaps between cases. Read without its context a ruling contradicts the basics — "new memos created don't need to be part of it" (2026-10-07, said of a `Loading` a held memo mounts — creation joins nothing; a memo joins by reading a held value, D-2) read, bare, as if a new async read on page B were not held with the navigation. Nothing in the archive says which rule wins when two seem to collide, so the most recent or most specific ruling was being treated as the authority. Here the fundamentals win, always.

## Vocabulary (visible terms only)

These are the only words the fundamentals use. Internal names (transaction, staging, seam, `_pendingValue`) belong to the mechanism paragraphs of the archive and to the code.

- **Screen.** The painted frame: what is shown. On screen means shown. A reader is not the screen, and neither is whether that reader holds (F-3). A removal that has committed can still be shown. Content behind a fallback is not shown; the fallback is. A value a hold has not revealed is not shown; the previous content is.
- **Reader.** Only a render effect is a reader. A memo, a user effect, an untracked read or a store proxy read is never a reader in its own right: a memo counts only through the render effects that read it, wherever it was created, and a pending value no render effect reaches holds nothing and reaches no boundary. "The reads under a boundary" always means the render effects under it.
- **Change.** A write (`set`, a store setter, an optimistic setter), the writes of an `action`, or an async answer landing. Several writes in one synchronous tick are one change.
- **Hold.** A change whose reveal is waiting on async work. While it holds, the screen keeps the content it showed before the change. The hold ends with one frame that reveals the change and everything that waited with it. (User-facing docs and diagnostics say _hold_, never _transaction_ or _transition_.)
- **Action.** `action(fn)`: the explicit hold — its writes are held until the body has returned and everything they put in flight has landed.
- **Boundary.** `<Loading>` (`createLoadingBoundary`). A boundary is **armed** until it has shown content (a _fresh_ boundary), and armed again by `on`; a boundary showing content is **disarmed** (a _shown_ boundary). Armed, it catches what is pending under it; disarmed, it forwards — it holds like any reader. `<Errored>` is the error boundary.
- **Fallback.** What a boundary displays instead of its content. Content behind a fallback is kept alive but is not on screen.
- **First load.** The wait before a source has its first answer. A first load is _loading_, not a change: there is no previous content to keep.
- **Question / re-ask.** A source's inputs are its question. An input changing asks a new question; `refresh()`, polling, an action's confirming refetch re-ask the same one.
- **Display-ahead reader.** `latest(x)`, `isPending(x)`, an optimistic value: a reader that shows a held change (or the verdict about it) before the hold reveals it.
- **Lane.** The world a display-ahead read opens: the screen plus what it shows ahead. What is rendered from a display-ahead read lives in its lane, and the lane holds _itself_ on its own async — a hold of its own, separate from the hold it reads ahead of (F-6).
- **Flush.** The unit of visibility. Writes become visible at the flush that carries them; a manual `flush()` ends a tick.

## F-1. The screen is whole

**Immutable.** Every frame the screen shows is consistent: a value and everything on screen derived from it changed in the same frame. The screen never shows a new value beside a stale derivation of it, and never a derivation ahead of its input. This holds for every kind of on-screen reader — a plain binding, a memo between, a boundary's content, a `latest()` view and what is rendered from it — and in every world the screen can show: the committed one, a held one, an optimistic one.

**What it does not say.** It does not say that two _unrelated_ changes must land together (F-2 decides what waits for what). It does not say that imperative, untracked reads are part of a frame — they read the screen as it stands (D-29, D-34). It does not say control and content must agree when the content is _absent_ by the author's choice (a fallback is content: F-4).

**Collapses:** A10 (`[isPending(x), x()]` atomic), A15's no-tearing core, A17's "no-tearing is an effect-level concern", A29/#3408 (`Panel: 1` beside `Count: 0` was the violation), #3479 "the frame is whole", #3851 (`content 1` beside `x = 0`), fuzzer finding F6 (#3811, the disarmed half — a lane revealing ahead of content a disarmed boundary is still showing), OL-R26, OS-R9, #3761 (ruled against), N1 (reverted). **Derived:** D-1–D-3, D-9, D-10, D-25–D-28, D-34. **Scenarios:** S-2–S-4, S-11, S-30, S-31, S-39.

## F-2. A change that needs async to be shown is held; the screen keeps what it showed

**Immutable.** When showing a change would put a render effect on screen that consumes async that has not answered, the change is _held_: nothing of it shows, the screen keeps the content it had, and when every such async has landed, the change and all of it reveal as one frame. Which async counts: any new async that render effect consumes, whatever its source, unless a boundary catches the render effect (F-3, F-4). A memo created during the hold is the same, when a render effect outside a boundary consumes it (D-2). This is the navigation model: click, page B's data loads, page B appears whole. An `action` is the explicit form: its writes are held until its body has returned and everything they put in flight has landed.

**What it does not say.** It does not say that what an armed boundary catches makes the change wait (F-4: the catch is the exception, by design). It does not say a new memo joins a hold by being created — it joins by reading a held value, sync or async (D-2); a memo that reads nothing held or pending shows now. It does not say a display-ahead reader holds (F-6).

**Collapses:** A15 (entanglement), A29 (the read entry, born held, ruling A), A26 (actions), A34 (1) (a write to a held slot joins), the tick ruling (2026-10-04), #3800's direction, the #2933 navigation model, recovery 2.1/3.1/3.2/4.2, the 2026-10-07 words "in general transitions can hold on any unready read". **Derived:** D-1–D-4, D-6–D-11, D-28. **Scenarios:** S-1–S-13, S-39.

## F-3. A reader holds only while it is the display

**Immutable.** A reader holds a change only while it is still the display of that read. A boundary catches a pending value when the render effect that reads it is under the boundary — where the memo was created does not matter, and a memo rendered by an effect outside the boundary is that effect's read, not the boundary's. A boundary showing its fallback is the display of everything under it: the content behind it is not on screen, those reads hold no change, and a change that only such content needed reveals at once — a transition's writes, or a lane's display-ahead value (D-21). A boundary output that has never committed shows nothing and holds nothing, and its readers wait with the mount's own hold. A reader outside the boundary that reads the same async still holds — one hold per reader that is still the display. An `on` reset moves the wait onto the boundary: the fallback stays until everything under it has settled, but the writes the reset released commit at once. A reader that is no longer the display holds nothing, whether or not it is still on screen: disposed, re-run without the read, or its removal committed. The error fallback is a fallback (F-12).

**What it does not say.** It does not say a fresh boundary's fallback is suppressed when something outside holds the same value (that was the dropped A35; F-4). It does not say a removal merely staged in a live hold has stopped being the display — that reader is still on screen, and it holds for every other change until the commit that removes it (D-9).

**Collapses:** A33 (#3375, #3459), §23.1 items 1–3 (2026-10-01; item 1: "participation is reads in render effects, not ownership"), A32 (children-forbidden readers see the frame), B5's "a fallback-caught flight holds nothing", recovery 2.2 and 9.5, O3 (a gated-away reader releases), #3426/#3372 (the last reader leaving releases), "a boundary output that never committed holds nothing" (2026-10-05). **Derived:** D-11, D-14, D-19, D-21, D-39. **Scenarios:** S-13, S-16, S-17, S-23, S-24.

## F-4. An armed boundary catches: what is pending under it holds nothing outside it

**Immutable.** A `<Loading>` is armed until it has shown content, and armed again by `on`. While it is armed, the render effects under it that are pending — on a first load, on a held value their content reads, on a new mount's async — are the boundary's, not any hold's: no change waits for them, whatever caused the mount (a click, a navigation, a flush, a root `render`) and whoever else on screen reads the same value; and the boundary displays its fallback in their place until they have settled, then the content — with the hold's commit for content that derives from a hold, at the landing for its own first load. Whether that fallback is ever _seen_ is a separate question with one answer: the fallback belongs to the frame of whatever armed the boundary — the mount, for a fresh boundary; the `on` change, for a re-arm — and appears when that frame lands. A boundary whose mount is held appears with that hold, fallback or content as the case is then.

**What it does not say.** It does not say a fallback is guaranteed to show — arming guarantees the catch, not the sight. It does not say an outside reader of the same value keeps an armed boundary from catching (the 2026-10-06 A35 draft and fuzzer rule MH8, dropped 2026-10-07). It does not say the root attach is deferred when there _is_ a boundary — the attach waits only for async nothing catches (D-6). It does not say an outside reader of the same value paints the fallback, on a fresh mount or a re-arm. The boundary has entered its fallback — the fallback is the display, whether or not it is shown — and that fallback is not shown while that reader holds. It is shown only if the hold ends before the content is ready (D-12, D-16).

**Collapses:** A29's boundary exemption (#3540, 2026-09-18), the boundary-scope ruling (2026-10-06) and #3824, the direction rule's fourth bullet (#3820), B-FRESH, B-FRAME and B-ROOT (recovery), recovery 1.1–1.4, 4.1/4.2 and 8.1, #3869's "a `Loading` a lane mounts over a flight shows its fallback". **Derived:** D-12, D-13, D-15, D-18, D-21, D-27, D-28. **Scenarios:** S-7, S-14, S-15, S-18, S-22, S-24, S-31, S-39.

## F-5. A disarmed boundary forwards: it keeps its content and holds like any reader, until `on` arms it again

**Immutable.** Once a `<Loading>` has shown content it is disarmed: a later refetch or hold does not send it back to its fallback — it keeps the content, and the pending render effects under it are forwarded into the change's hold exactly as if they were read in the open (F-2). The only way back is `on` — a dependency list: a change to anything `on` reads arms the boundary again (F-4), so the render effects under it leave the hold they were forwarded into and the boundary shows its fallback, with the change's frame, until the new content is ready; if nothing under it is pending, nothing happens. The value `on` returns is never compared, and the children are not re-created — they stay alive behind the fallback. A lane is that same hold: the async under a disarmed boundary is forwarded into it, so a display-ahead value and the content move together, exactly as they would with no boundary there.

**What it does not say.** It does not say the fallback of a re-arm appears _ahead_ of the change that caused it (F-7: it lands with that change's frame). It does not say a re-arm paints its fallback while its frame waits on the same data read outside the boundary. The boundary is armed; the screen stays on the old content until that hold lifts (D-16).

**Collapses:** §23.1 item 4 ("fallback only on the uninitialized first load and an `on` reset"), A33's reset half (the hold moves onto the boundary), the fuzzer-finding F6 ruling's showing-content half (2026-10-05, #3811 — a disarmed boundary forwards a lane), B-KEEP and B-ON (recovery), recovery 2.1 and 5.1, the `createLoadingBoundary` JSDoc, `05-async-data.md` "`Loading` `on` prop". **Derived:** D-14–D-18. **Scenarios:** S-16–S-22, S-24 (the disarmed row).

## F-6. Display-ahead readers show now and never hold the change they read ahead of; their own async holds them, in their own lane

**Immutable.** `latest(x)`, `isPending(x)` and an optimistic value are the screen's way of showing a held change ahead of its reveal: they display the in-flight value, or the verdict about it, now, beside the held screen, and they are never a reason for the change to wait. But a display-ahead read is a world of its own — a _lane_ — and F-1 and F-2 apply inside it exactly as they do outside: **async derived from a display-ahead read opens a hold of its own, in that lane.** An async memo over `latest(x)`, a refetch an optimistic value feeds, a derivation of either that is rendered — while it is in flight, the display-ahead readers and everything rendered from them wait together, showing what the screen showed, and reveal together when it lands: never a `latest()` view beside a half-computed derivation of itself. That lane hold is separate from the parent hold on both sides — the parent does not wait for it, and it does not wait for the parent; it reveals at its own landing. `latest()` escapes the hold, not its own lane. A display-ahead read in `on` arms the boundary from a frame that has already landed, so the fallback shows now, beside the held frame; every read of the held value stays held (D-17).

**What it does not say.** It does not say display-ahead readers never wait — they wait for their _own_ async, in their own lane; what they never wait for is the hold they are reading ahead of. It does not say `latest()` shows a write before the flush that carries it (F-10). It does not say a display-ahead reader can _reveal_ a held change for the rest of the screen — a mount it opens is a mainline mount and reads the screen (D-27). It does not say an optimistic value is pending (F-9: verdict-inert).

**Collapses:** A17 and A18 (c)/(d) (the override is the displayed value), A19's display-ahead verdict, §20.1 "verdicts are guesses the system supplies", #3444 (`latest` peers through), #3460 (a lane is a transition seen from outside), the lanes corollary of A15, "a verdict reader's own async is held" (§20.1 question 3), recovery 6.1/6.2, B-AHEAD. **Derived:** D-12, D-17, D-21, D-22–D-27, D-31, D-37. **Scenarios:** S-21, S-24–S-31, S-40.

## F-7. Nothing else reveals ahead of the hold it belongs to

**Immutable.** Content that derives from a held change — a derivation of it, a new mount's derived content, a boundary's content under it — belongs to that hold and appears with its commit, not before, even where showing it early would tear nothing visible. A change's consequences land in the change's frame: now when nothing holds that frame, with the commit when something does — the fallback swap an `on` change causes lands with that change's frame, the content a navigation re-asks lands with the navigation. The one exception is the author's visible declaration: display-ahead readers (F-6), including a boundary armed by one. A fallback is never an exception — it belongs to the frame of whatever armed the boundary (F-4), never to the hold its content reads, so it shows nothing of that hold ahead of it; what it shows ahead is only that the content is pending, which is a verdict, and verdicts are F-6's.

**What it does not say.** It does not say a separate write joins the hold. It does not say a fallback is "revealed ahead" — there is no frame in which a fallback shows a value from the hold its content reads.

**Collapses:** A29's "a value derived from the held world is that transaction's work, whichever path first read it", B-FRAME, #3575's frame-following `on`, "membership in the transaction is the criterion, not data lineage" (recovery 3.2), recovery 4.1/4.2/5.2, #3800. **Derived:** D-1, D-2, D-13, D-15, D-16. **Scenarios:** S-1, S-2, S-6, S-15, S-18.

## F-9. `isPending` is about a new question; a re-ask is quiet; a first load is loading, not pending

**Immutable.** `isPending(x)` is `true` iff the value you can see for `x` is not the final answer to a _new question_: an input changed and the new answer has not revealed, a landed answer waits in a hold, or in-flight work declared it will change the data (`affects()`). It is `false` the moment the value you see is final. Re-asking the same question — `refresh()`, polling, an action's confirming refetch — is quiet: the shown value still answers, `isPending` stays `false`, and the fresh value reveals silently; to make a reload read as pending, declare it (`affects(x); refresh(x)`). Before a first answer there is no visible value to be non-final: an uninitialized source is _loading_ — its reads suspend to a boundary and `isPending` of it is `false` — and a declared first paint (`loadingValue`) is loading too. Optimistic values are verdict-inert: an optimistic write never makes its own slot pending and never silences anything. Pending is per node — per store key — never store-wide. A reader behind a fallback is not the display (F-3), so that node is not pending: `isPending` of it is false, and `latest` of it is the value it has produced (D-22).

**What it does not say.** It does not say `isPending` can be `true` on creation (it cannot — hydration parity). It does not say an action in progress is pending data (process affordances are co-written state, not verdicts).

**Collapses:** A19 (three causes, two exceptions), A24 (question-scoped; `affects`), A27 (the loading window), A7, A8 (per channel), A9, A12, A13, A14, A16, A22, A23, OL-R12, OS-R17/R40, PJ-R28–R31, recovery 6.3/6.4. **Derived:** D-22–D-26, D-30, D-36. **Scenarios:** S-14, S-25–S-29, S-36, S-37.

## F-10. A write becomes visible at flush — to every channel

**Immutable.** Between `set(x)` and the flush that carries it, nothing shows the write: not the screen, not `latest(x)`, not `isPending(x)`, not an untracked read, not a derivation. Only the writer's own composition channels see it — an updater function's `prev`, a store setter's draft, an `affects()` declaration walk in the same tick. `flush()` is the boundary; inside a computation or a flush, every write issued is promoted at its end, so the graph never runs a round against a value it wrote but cannot see. An optimistic write is a write: it is the displayed value from the flush that carries it, not before.

**What it does not say.** It does not say a held write is invisible after its flush — `latest()` and `isPending()` show it then (F-6).

**Collapses:** A28 (1)–(5), O4, A26's "unflushed" reads, CS-R24/R25, OL-R2 and OS-R1 (superseded 2026-09-10). **Derived:** D-7, D-8, D-22, D-32. **Scenarios:** S-8, S-10, S-25, S-32.

## F-11. An optimistic value is a guess over a hold: the displayed value until that hold commits or its own source answers differently

**Immutable.** An optimistic write is a guess over the hold of the frame it is made in. From the flush that carries it, the guess is _the_ value of its slot for the screen and for untracked reads, for as long as that hold is open; with nothing held to be optimistic over, the write is as if it never happened. When the slot's own source answers, an equal answer confirms silently and a different answer corrects: the screen keeps what it showed until the hold commits, `latest()` answers the truth, `isPending()` reads `true` while they differ, and the correction reveals atomically with the hold's other writes. A newer hold's guess on a slot wins over an older hold's late answer. The plain writes held in the same frame wait (F-2) while the guess shows now (F-6); at the commit, the guess's revert and the held writes' reveal land as one frame.

**What it does not say.** It does not say an optimistic value survives the hold it was made over — an `action` is only the usual way to keep one open. It does not say a guess the screen never showed stays readable at its correction (it is void; untracked reads see the screen — D-34). It does not say an optimistic write is pending (F-9).

**Collapses:** A17, A18 (lifetime, supersession, provenance Q-D, body-end #3427, "correction display is what showed" 2026-10-05), the lane contracts (2026-10-01/03), O1 (same-tick adoption), OL-R5/R6/R8/R16/R20/R26/R28, OS-R10/R14/R20/R39/R42. **Derived:** D-7, D-32–D-37. **Scenarios:** S-32–S-35.

## F-12. An error is a boundary returning to its fallback; pending is not an error

**Immutable.** An error thrown by content under an `<Errored>` sends that boundary to its error fallback, which is then the display (F-3): readers behind it hold nothing. A pending read is never an error: `<Errored>` does not catch it and never shows its fallback for a wait, and `ASYNC_OUTSIDE_LOADING_BOUNDARY` is a warning, not a failure. An error that escapes every boundary halts the reactive system (`REACTIVITY_HALTED`); later writes are ignored. In effects, compute-phase errors can be intercepted (`createEffect`'s error arm) and are logged and skipped when they are not; effect-phase errors escalate to the nearest boundary.

**What it does not say.** It does not say an error clears on the next write — the error fallback stays until a new answer replaces the errored one (D-39).

**Collapses:** A1–A6, §23.1 items 2–3 (2026-10-01), the 7b re-pin (2026-10-02), recovery 9.1/9.2. **Derived:** D-38, D-39. **Scenarios:** S-38.

## Reading the fundamentals together

- **What waits:** F-2 says a change waits for the async its on-screen content needs. F-3 removes a reader that is no longer the display. F-4 removes what an armed boundary catches from it — a transition's reads, or a lane's own async — and the boundary shows a fallback in its place. F-6 removes display-ahead readers from it.
- **What shows meanwhile:** F-1 says whatever shows is a whole frame. F-5 says a disarmed boundary's old content is that frame's. F-6 says display-ahead readers are the one way to show the change early; they bring their own derivations with them and hold themselves, in their own lane, on their own async. F-7 says nothing else from the change shows early.
- **Verdicts and visibility:** F-9 says when the screen admits it is stale. F-10 says when a write exists at all. F-11 says what an optimistic value is and for how long. F-12 says what an error is and is not.

A ruling that fits none of these twelve is a finding: either a thirteenth fundamental is missing, or the ruling is a mistake. `RULES-DERIVED.md` lists the candidates found so far under "Flagged".
